# Technical risk register (v0.1, 2026-11-13; status updated through phase 5 part 1, 2026-11-19)

Likelihood and impact: H / M / L. "Phase" is where the mitigation lands (see ARCHITECTURE §14).
The two risks that most threaten the project's *conclusions*, rather than its code, are K1 and K2.

## Status during phase 5 (part 1, 2026-11-17 to 19)

| ID | Change |
|---|---|
| K3 optimistic passive backtests | **Mitigation built, checked on simulated and on real data.** **Equities (Nasdaq BX ITCH, one day):** on 801,154 orders of the 8 most-traded stocks the model is never optimistic: no false fill, no early first fill, no overfill. That needed two corrections, both prompted by this check (K28). It eventually finds 93% of the real volume, though only 7% of first fills land in the right nanosecond. **Real fills (2026-11-18):** each of 172,862 resting Bitstamp orders was replayed through the stream without itself, in four variants (HISTORICAL_DATA §6). In every variant the model filled none of the 172,564 that never traded, no order earlier than it really filled, and none for more. The check found one optimistic rule and it was fixed: a trade-through filled a copy's whole rest, crediting 0.72 BTC that never traded through. Recovery depends on taker information. The conservative rules alone find a third of real fills on time. A taker-remainder rule (Bitstamp publishes takers' orders; the premise is checked per taker and held for all 79 with a remainder), scored against history rebuilt without each order, finds 292 of 298 and 99.6% of the volume. 71% of the filled volume would otherwise have stayed with the takers, so on this venue maker-only data (LOBSTER-style) would badly understate passive fills. **Simulated data:** the pure-replay queue model is FIFO and conservative ([HISTORICAL_DATA.md](HISTORICAL_DATA.md) §4). Against the exchange's own fills of about 3,700 probe orders (both backgrounds, 1- and 5-share probes) it filled no order the exchange did not, and none earlier. It found every fill that a probe-free stream can show at the same nanosecond. **New from real data:** Bitstamp publishes every incoming order as resting before its outcome. A rebuilt book was crossed 2,974 times in 19 minutes, each time for less than one millisecond stamp, and 91% of those orders were MOC orders deleted untraded (HISTORICAL_DATA §6). A replay must not fill against an order until its millisecond has settled; the LOBSTER export never shows takers as resting, and copies join only after their entry stamp. *Still open:* impact (replay cannot show reactions to our orders; the counterfactual assumes none), L2-only data (queue position must be assumed), hidden orders, and feeds without taker orders (LOBSTER, ITCH), where only the conservative rules apply. |
| K16 unverified formulas or claims | The LOBSTER format comes from secondary sources: a reconstructor library's documentation, a loader script and a course page. The empty-level dummy prices and halt codes are from memory, and the reader does not depend on them. LOBSTER's own page could not be loaded. The first real file will confirm or correct them (HISTORICAL_DATA §1). **Bitstamp:** the channel formats and subtype codes were read in its WebSocket documentation. The documentation names subtype 5 only "MOC"; that it means maker-or-cancel rests on the observed behaviour. The millisecond resolution of order-event stamps is measured, not documented. **ITCH 5.0:** the layouts are from memory [M], but every message type's length matches the BX file's own length prefixes, and the book rebuild finds no inconsistency in 29.2 million messages (HISTORICAL_DATA §7). The specification text was not read. |
| K11 data licensing | The Bitstamp recording (200 MB raw) lives in `data/bitstamp/`, and the Nasdaq BX ITCH sample day (391 MB, 863 MB decompressed) in `data/itch/`. Neither source's redistribution terms were checked. Raw data stays out of the published repository (`/data/` is ignored); derived statistics are fine. |
| **K26 (new): tiny aggressive orders** | In the simulated market an aggressive order executes a median of 2 shares (90th percentile 3, none 6 or more), against a top-of-book depth of hundreds of shares. Real markets differ, so any result that depends on aggressor size, such as replay blind spots, queue-depletion speed or impact per order, inherits it. L: H, I: M. *Mitigation:* treat as part of K1. Compare with the order-size distribution in real MBO data once available, and recalibrate the background if needed. Real order-level data now exists, but only from a crypto venue whose sizes do not map onto the simulated equity-like market. The comparison has not been made. |
| **K27 (new): one venue's quirks** | Real data comes from two venues, one window each. Bitstamp BTC/USD, 19 minutes: MOC orders are 95% of creations, event stamps are whole milliseconds, and 13% of snapshots are at least 1 ms stale. Nasdaq BX, one day (2019-12-30): 2,686 executions (0.5%) are of orders ranked at a better price than they display (K28), and other venues may rank or report such orders differently. Neither transfers to other venues, and BX is a small one; Nasdaq's main market was not checked. L: H, I: M. *Mitigation:* state the venue and window in every result (provenance does). Add days and venues (Nasdaq's main-market ITCH files are 3.5–16 GB) before any general equity claim. |
| **K28 (closed 2026-11-19): displayed price is not ranked price** | *First reported as* "priority is not strict FIFO on BX": in 0.2% of executions a newer order passed an older one at the same displayed price, and there the replay model made 539 false fills among 710,970 never-filled orders. *Cause:* those orders were ranked at a better price than they displayed, under BX's Price to Comply rule. Their executions arrive as ITCH "executed with price", one increment better than displayed. Every execution at a displayed price followed arrival order (HISTORICAL_DATA §7). *Fixed:* such executions are written in LOBSTER form at the price they traded at, and the replay model fills nothing from them. A copy also no longer sees the nanosecond its original was cancelled in, which removed the last 33. Now no false, early or excess fill on 801,154 orders. *Residual:* a LOBSTER file that writes these executions at the displayed price would bring the false fills back; check that when a real LOBSTER file is read. L: L, I: M. *Lesson:* priority holds at the price a venue ranks an order at, which can differ from the price it displays. |
| **K29 (new, closed 2026-11-19): outdated binary behind the Python wrappers** | The Python wrappers built `hftsim` only when it was missing, so after a source change they silently ran the old binary. Found when a rerun of the ITCH check after the K28 fix reproduced the old report exactly. *Fixed:* they ask cargo once per process. Cargo leaves an up-to-date binary untouched, so the run cache (keyed on the binary) stays valid. Tested. *Lesson:* as with K22, an artefact that exists is not proof it is current. |

| K7 I/O and memory | Every run keeps the full event log in memory for the metrics (about 430 MB for 180 simulated seconds with 30 informed traders). With other work sharing the laptop, that limits the batch to 4 parallel runs. **Event-level output now exists** as opt-in Parquet through the bindings: 3.0 million events of a 180 s run come to 24 MB with zstd, written after the run, off the simulation path. *Open:* recording levels or streaming metrics, to cut the in-memory log. |
| K6 Python agents | Still open. The bindings run whole markets; a Python agent inside the event loop needs a safe adapter around `sim::Agent` that is not built. |
| K19 agent-id streams | The runner adds the background first and strategy agents after it (informed, arbitrageurs, market makers). The background's random streams are therefore the same in every cell of a grid for a given seed, which is what common random numbers need. A market maker's id moves when the number of informed traders changes. Its quoting draws no random numbers, but its link-latency draws are keyed by id, so across such cells only the background's randomness is common. |
| K21 background mean reversion | **Re-measured.** Without market makers, the default background's lag-1 autocorrelation of 1 s returns is −0.21 [−0.25, −0.16] (8 seeds), and −0.09 with 30 informed traders; zero intelligence is −0.05 (interval includes zero). With one to four step-7 market makers it is −0.07 to +0.08, and every interval includes zero ([RESEARCH_RESULTS.md](RESEARCH_RESULTS.md), RQ8). The makers earn no more on the default background than on zero intelligence, and every phase-4 finding holds on both backgrounds. |
| K23 no informed flow | **Measured, and partly wrong as stated.** Even without informed traders, adverse selection at 1 s costs a GLFT maker 55–60% of its spread capture: the background's split metaorders are persistent one-sided flow. With 60 precise informed traders the figure is 90–97%. Still, the maker stays profitable in nearly every configuration (RQ2), and the informed traders lose money as a group on the default background. The simulated market remains generous to passive liquidity, and only historical mark-outs (phase 5) can say whether real flow is. |
| **K24 (new, closed): race undercount** | A cancel that arrives after its order was filled is refused at the gateway, which already knows of the fill, and never reaches the engine log. Race detection from the engine log alone missed exactly the maker's losing message in a sniping race. *Fixed:* the kernel records the gateway's own answers, and races include them (tested end to end). |
| **K25 (new, closed): PnL window** | Agent PnL from the ledger covered the warm-up, while the market metrics did not. *Fixed:* a windowed decomposition carries in the position held at the window start at that moment's mid. It is exact against the ledger (tested). |

## Research validity

| ID | Risk | L | I | Mitigation | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **The simulated market is not realistic.** Zero-intelligence or hand-made agent ecologies miss real order-flow structure (long-memory order signs, clustered arrivals, intraday seasonality, hidden liquidity), so market-making results reflect model artefacts. | H | H | Calibrate background flow to stylised facts before any strategy study (spread distribution, cancel-to-trade ratios by distance from the touch, order-sign autocorrelation, square-root impact). Consider queue-reactive or Hawkes generators. Report sensitivity to the agent mix. | 2 |
| K2 | **False profitability.** Strategies tuned in simulation exploit simulator quirks (no fees, perfect fills, predictable agents, known auction times). | H | H | Out-of-sample seeds and agent populations; fees and rebates in accounting; randomised auction ends; paired comparisons with confidence intervals; never call a strategy profitable from simulation alone; separate historical-validation stage. | 3–5 |
| K3 | **Historical backtests of passive strategies are optimistic.** In L2 data your queue position is unknown; MBO data lacks hidden and iceberg orders and true participant latency; replay cannot show how the market would have reacted to your orders. | H | H | Prefer MBO data; explicit queue-position fill models with conservative assumptions (fill only after the queue ahead is depleted); hybrid replay for impact; state the data level in every result; report fill-model sensitivity. | 5 |
| K4 | **Latency model misspecified.** Real latency has heavy tails, bursts, correlated jitter, per-session ordering and gateway queueing; a constant delay misstates race outcomes. | M | H | Distribution library (base + heavy-tailed jitter + rare spikes), per-link FIFO, gateway service queue, per-subscriber feed delays; magnitudes anchored to published figures; sensitivity sweeps over latency rank (Baron et al.: relative latency is what matters). | 1 |
| K10 | **Our rulebook is one venue design.** Results on priority, self-trade handling or auction pricing may not transfer to a real venue (e.g. Nasdaq replace priority differs from CME's). | M | M | Documented rulebook with venue precedents; make rules switchable (replace-priority convention, pro-rata allocation, price protection) before venue-specific studies. | 3+ |
| K16 | **Unverified formulas or claims get implemented.** Several formulas (A–S details, GLFT closed form, CKS OFI, Rule 605 definitions) and exchange rules are snippet-level only. | M | H | Formula register (RESEARCH_SURVEY §D): transcribe from the primary paper and test against an independent numerical solution before use. | 3–4 |

## Engineering

| ID | Risk | L | I | Mitigation | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K5 | **Determinism erodes** as layers are added: floating point in agents (libm differences, FMA, parallel reductions), hash iteration, wall-clock reads, thread scheduling. | M | H | Integer core; deterministic hashers; one thread per simulation; per-entity RNG streams; no wall clock in `lob` or `sim`; event digests in CI; cross-platform reproducibility promised only for the integer core. | 1–4 |
| K6 | **Python agents are slow.** PyO3 callbacks cost microseconds each, so a Python agent on every event caps simulation speed. | H | M | Core agents in Rust; Python agents on timers or batched events; vectorised interfaces for research code. | 2, 4 |
| K7 | **I/O dominates runtime.** Logging every event (64 bytes × millions per run) and book snapshots to disk can exceed simulation cost. | M | M | Columnar compressed storage (Parquet); recording levels (events / BBO / sampled depth); write off the simulation thread. | 4 |
| K13 | **Accounting overflow.** Notional = price × quantity in `i64` overflows for fine price units (e.g. crypto) and long runs. | M | H | `i128` (or checked arithmetic) for notional, cash and PnL; spec bounds already cap price at 2^61 and order size at 2^40. | 1–2 |
| K14 | **Market orders sweep thin synthetic books** (no price protection), producing unrealistic crashes. | M | M | CME-style protection bands and limit-price collars in the engine; gateway risk limits per agent. | 1–2 |
| K15 | **Agent ecologies become unstable** (empty book, runaway prices, liquidity spirals) and waste runs. | M | M | Run monitors (one-sided book duration, spread blow-ups); kill switches via `CancelAll`; scenario-level invariants; automatic volatility halts. | 2 |
| K17 | **Memory-bound at depth.** Median cost rises 3.5× from a 1k-order to a 190k-order book (cache misses on the arena and hash map). | M | L | Fine for research scale. If needed: direct id-indexed slot table (ids are sequential), smaller nodes, locality-aware slot reuse, per-level contiguous storage. | later |
| K18 | **One engine per instrument.** Cross-instrument strategies (statistical arbitrage) need several books with consistent event ordering. | M | M | Multi-book scheduler in the kernel with one global `(time, seq)` order. | 1 |
